ABSTRACT
BackgroundIdentifying the factors affecting emotional divorce due to the negative consequences of this social problem in women can play an important role in preventing its occurrence. Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the relationship between internet addiction, domestic violence, and emotional divorce among married women in Tehran.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted among married women in Tehran, Iran. Sampling was conducted first through a multi-stage cluster method and then continued using a random method. Tehran was divided into five parts: central, northern, southern, eastern, and western. The sample size was divided by 5, resulting in 80 participants selected from each part. In each part, two regions were selected, and in each region, 40 houses were randomly selected. Researchers visited the selected houses, and the questionnaires were filled out at home by the women. Tools such as Gottman’s Emotional Divorce Test, Young’s Internet Addiction Test, and the Domestic Violence against Women Survey were used to collect data. Descriptive and inferential statistics were used to analyze the data in SPSS (version 24) software.ResultsA total of 400 women participated in the study. 46% of all married women suffered emotional divorce. The analysis of these tests showed that are positive and significant relationships between emotional divorce and internet addiction (r = 0.626, p < 0.0001), emotional divorce and domestic violence (r = 0.632, p < 0.0001), and internet addiction and domestic violence (r = 0.455, p < 0.0001). It was also found that there are significant negative relationships between emotional divorce and both the level of education (r=-0.115, p = 0.021) and employment status (r=-0.512, p < 0.0001). Additionally, there are significant negative relationships between internet addiction and age (r=-0.285, p < 0.0001), duration of marriage (r=-0.237, p < 0.0001) and employment status (r=-0.694, p < 0.0001). Furthermore, it was revealed that there are significant negative relationship between domestic violence and age (r=-0.104, p = 0.037) and level of education (r=-0.156, p = 0.002).ConclusionAlmost half of the married women in this study experienced emotional divorce, internet addiction, and domestic violence. These factors have a direct relationship with emotional divorce. Future studies are needed to understand how internet addiction might be associated with separation, emotional divorce and family problems.

BODY
Introduction

Humans are inherently social creatures. Human communication and group formation are the most important goals in their social lives. One of the most important aspects of social life is to form a family. Family, as the first system, is a general and global institution that is necessary to meet the critical and emotional needs of humans and to survive the society [1]. Communication takes place in a society based on family relations and the more appropriate, the relationship among family members within the family is, the more stable, the family will be [2]. Women are considered as one of the important pillars and as the epicenter of love in the family and is the first family member to have a direct relationship with her children. Paying attention to women in families is important [3, 4]. A family is formed by marriage and faces disintegration with divorce. Divorce is a starting point of individual, family and social collapses and its negative impacts on women are more than men. According to experts, divorce is a process that its steps are minor differences, repeated and serious differences, the reluctance of one spouse to another one, long huff, being tired of each other and conflict, emotional divorce, and legal divorce [5, 6].

Emotional divorce and silent separation is a type of divorce that is not still recognized legally and judicially. According to some considerations, the couple has to live together apparently but because of the cold relationship and non-exchange of emotions, a lot of dissatisfaction with frustration can be seen in them [7]. Emotional divorce directly impacts official or legal divorce. Emotional divorce has many consequences and the main victims of this type of divorce are children. In a house where the relationships between couples are cold and lifeless and there is no affection between them, in the permanent process, children witness the disrespect of their parents and, replacement of affection with aggression and violence. In these circumstances, it is expected that children, like their parents, retire into their shells. In other words, social pathologists believe that the largest number of delinquents in society are the same children [4, 8].

Pachauri P, in his study, concluded that young couples need to manage and restore the relations between themselves. Cold relations between couples are mainly due to economic problems and not meeting their expectations [9]. Shakeri et al., in their studies, referred to relational variables, including size, support, verification, cohesion, connection strength, intimacy, common activities, and closeness centrality and overlapping as effective factors for intimacy with a spouse and also, noted that intimacy with a spouse has the greatest impact in removing emotional divorce. Also, they referred to attribute variables, including number of children, duration of marriage, knowing the spouse before marriage, having relative with a spouse, affect intimacy with the spouse and emotional divorce [10, 11].

“Robert Sternberg” refers to the different aspects of love with a triangle that has three components: commitment, intimacy, passion, and desire. The three components of love are combined in numerous ways and according to Sternberg, eight types of relationships are identified. By considering three components of love identified by Sternberg, since emotional divorce is a lack of intimacy, two relationships defined by Sternberg, i.e. empty love and foolish love can be called emotional divorce [12, 13]. So, identifying effective factors and investigating causal and mediator situations in the occurrence of emotional divorce seems necessary [4, 14]. In the occurrence of emotional divorce, a series of factors such as not meeting emotional needs, neglect of male to female, communication problems, intellectual and attitude disharmony between spouses, lack of empathy and companionship, excessive work of one of the spouses, economic problems, impaired sexual relations, cultural and training differences between spouses, having personal entertainment and other personality traits such as selfishness, greed, pride and stubbornness, low self-confidence and self-esteem, addiction to virtual networks and the internet, can be noted [15–18].

Now, attendance in virtual networks and using internet networks in the world is growing incredibly. Despite its abundant advantages, the widespread use of the internet can cause many problems for couples, for example, easy access to sexual content and images, non-confidentiality of information, and addiction to this type of communication in internet networks [19]. Internet addiction is placed in the area of ​​impulse control disorders, including an individual’s inability to resist his/her impulse to engage in a particular behavior despite considering its consequences. Malak believes that, like other types of addiction, internet addiction is associated with symptoms such as anxiety, depression, irritability, obsessive ideologies, isolation, and fragmentation of social relations [20]. The term “Internet addiction disorder”, which is a behavioral disorder, is used as the exposure mechanism and it is derived from the drug dependence criteria of DSM-V [21]. Ivan Goldberg called this type of addiction “Internet addiction” that at the micro level, it causes ignorance of family ties and breakdown of social relations [22]. At the macro level, it can cause social crisis such as social apathy. So it can be said that adverse consequences of internet addiction are obvious in three physical, mental, and social areas [23]. According to the latest studies conducted in Iran, the majority of internet users are young people and 35% of their dialogue in virtual spaces, 28% of them play computer games, 30% of them review their e-mails and 25% of them do searching in worldwide websites [24]. Although the share of women and their addiction is not specified in these statistics, the greatest impact of consequences of internet addiction has been observed on women. This can cause emotional divorce itself [23, 25].

According to the World Health Organization, mistreatment or violence is defined as the intentional use of physical force or power, or threats on others, which can be without apparent damage or associated with injury, psychological trauma, multiple deprivation, and even death [26]. Violence is introduced as a phenomenon that is a major obstacle to achieving equality, development, and peace. According to the global report, rape, and domestic violence, compared to diseases such as breast and cervical cancer and accidents, cause more harm to women’s health [27]. According to the American Court of Justice, women are eleven times more exposed to domestic violence than men. Violence against women in the family is the most common type of violence against women and it occurs by the closest family member such as the husband [16, 28, 29].

There are unique social and cultural conditions in Iran, where family formation has always been crucial in the individual and social lives of Iranians. The common social and religious values in Iranian society have consistently emphasized the sanctity of the family and the importance of forming, maintaining, and strengthening it. Meanwhile divorce is viewed as one of the most negative and inappropriate social phenomena in Iranian society, with significant consequences for couples, especially women. As a result, many women prefer to continue living with their husbands, often in a state of emotional divorce, to avoid the social repercussions of divorce. Common reasons for not pursuing divorce include fear of family and social rejection, lack of economic independence, having children, and the difficulties of separation from their children after divorce [4, 30]. On the other hand, the behavioral issues contributing to these family problems have not been thoroughly explored in Iran. Moreover, some family challenges have emerged due to recently introduced technologies, like widespread access to the internet and internet addiction. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the relationship between internet addiction, domestic violence, and emotional divorce among married women in Tehran, Iran.

Methods

Study population and sample size

A cross-sectional survey was conducted to investigate the relationship between internet addiction, domestic violence, and emotional divorce among married women in Tehran, Iran. The study population consisted of married women living with their husbands in Tehran in 2020. According to the Iran Statistics Center, the number of married women in Tehran was 2,800,000. Using a significance level of 0.05 and a confidence coefficient of 95% (Z = 1.96), we considered a margin of error d = 0.05, and the probability of occurrence and non-occurrence of emotional divorce trait was setup at p = 0.5. This value was chosen due to the lack of available statistics on this matter; thus, we used 0.5 for both probability cases to calculate the maximum required sample size. By applying these numbers in Cochran’s sampling formula, the desired sample number was determined to be 384 participants. Sampling was initially conducted using a multi-stage cluster method and was then continued with a random selection process. In order to ensure a sufficient sample size, 420 subjects were enrolled; however, 20 were excluded from the study due to officially initiating divorce proceedings. Consequently, the study was conducted with a final sample of 400 participants.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were married women who lived in Tehran at the time of the study, for at least one year together with their spouse, had a good mental health and were able to read and write. The exclusion criteria was: unwillingness to continue participating in the research.

Sampling and data collection

Sampling was initially done in a multi-stage cluster and continued by random selection process. For this purpose, the city of Tehran was divided into 5 regions: central, northern, southern, eastern and western. By dividing the sample size by 5, we selected 80 participants from each region. Two areas were chosen within each region, and then 40 households were randomly selected from each area. Interviewers visited the selected households and provided necessary explanations about the study. Participants who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study after completing the informed consent form and were asked to fill out the questionnaires.

Study tools

Gottman emotional divorce questionnaire

The Emotional Divorce Questionnaire was developed by Gottman in 2000 and translated and validated by Jazayeri in Iran [31]. The questionnaire is organized into 24 items in the form of two options yes (score 1) or no (score zero) and includes sentences about different aspects of married life that the person may agree or disagree with. The minimum score obtained by the subject will be zero and the maximum score will be 24. After collecting the answers, if the number of positive answers is equal to 8 or higher, it means that the person’s married life is subject to separation and there are signs of emotional divorce in him [31]. This questionnaire was approved by experts and to obtain the validity of the structure, factor analysis was used by Varimax rotation method and thus four factors were identified: separation and distance from each other, feeling of loneliness and isolation, need for companionship and all the talk and the feeling of boredom and restlessness. The values ​​of participation and factor load of the questionnaire questions showed that the factor load of all questions ranged from 0.49 to 0.80, which have an acceptable factor load [31]. The total reliability of the scale in the present study using Cronbach’s alpha was 0.78.

Young internet addiction questionnaire

The Internet Addiction Questionnaire was developed by Kimberly Young in 1999 [32]. This questionnaire consists of 20 items and is evaluated using a 5-point Likert scale (never = 0, rarely = 1, occasionally = 2, often = 3, frequency = 4, always = 5). The minimum score obtained by the subject is zero and the maximum score is 100, with a higher score indicating greater dependence on the internet. The status of users is determined based on a score below 49 (normal user), 79 − 50 (mild addiction), 100 − 79 (severe addiction) to the internet. Validity and reliability of the Internet Addiction Questionnaire was obtained in one study as follows: The validity of the questionnaire by simultaneous validity method was equal to r = 0.81, differential validity r = 0.62 and three types of reliability = 0.7 α, halving was r = 0.64 and retest was r = 0.47 [21]. The total scale reliability in the present study was 0.94 using Cronbach’s alpha.

Violence against women questionnaire

The Violence against Women Questionnaire was developed by Haj Yahia in 2001 [33]. The Tolman Women’s Psychological Violence Questionnaire [34], and the Hudson and McIntosh spouse-abuse scale [35] were used to prepare this questionnaire. This questionnaire was translated by Khaghani Far at Al-Zahra University and its validity was confirmed by several expert professors. The Violence against Women Questionnaire consists of 32 questions and 4 subscales (psychological violence 1–16, physical violence 17–17, sexual violence 28–30 and economic violence 31 and 32). Based on the results of the first Palestinian survey on violence against women, which was conducted on 2410 women, the internal consistency coefficient or Cronbach’s alpha for the four factors of Haj Yahia test were 0.71, 0.86, 0.93, 0.92 are obtained. The total scale reliability in the present study was 0.92 using Cronbach’s alpha.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed after collecting them using SPSS software version 24 and using the methods of normal distribution of variables, mean comparison, analysis of variance, correlation analysis, logistic regression analysis if the conditions are met.

Ethical considerations

The present study is part of a dissertation (ethics code: IR.USWR.REC.1395.244) in social work approved by the Ethics Committee of University of Social Welfare and Rehabilitation Sciences. Informed consent was obtained from all participants. All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. In this study, an effort was made to observe the principle of confidentiality and respect for human beings, and if the samples were curious about the research results, their email or phone numbers were taken so that the results could be sent to them.

Discussion

The aim of this study was to investigate the relationship between internet addiction, domestic violence, and emotional divorce among married women in Tehran. The results showed that there is a significant and positive relationship between internet addiction and emotional divorce suggesting that internet addiction can increase emotional divorce by increasing social networks, reducing intimacy between the spouses, and increasing the distance between them. The findings are consistent with previous studies by Besra [23], Khanmohamadi [7], Shakeri [10] and Rezaei’s [19]. In these studies it was found that, the variables of order within the family, closeness, and intimacy of family members, family power structure, and social capital within the family have the highest significant relationships with emotional divorce and whenever family structure balance is shattered under the influence of internal and external conditions, their effective strengthening functions such as social capital within family, order within the family and power structure are challenged and this prepares the ground of emotional divorce. Internet addiction phenomenon can cause cold relations by increasing social networks, reducing intimacy between couples and thereby disrupting the order within the family leading to emotional divorce.

Previous studies by YaghoubiHasankola [25] and Movahhed [18], have shown that the internet al.one cannot contribute to the collapse of the family. In fact, gaps and conflicts in couples’ relationships, which appear in different ways, become the basis for internet addiction, ultimately resulting in further deterioration of the relationship and, eventually, its collapse. The internet and virtual space, in conjunction with the daily challenges of married life, play three main roles in family breakdown: the role of aggravation, the role of facilitation, and the role of acceleration. Based on research findings, there is a significant and positive relationship between domestic violence and emotional divorce among married women. So, it can be said that an increase in domestic violence is associated with the increase in emotional divorce and vice versa. The findings are consistent with Tonkonogy [16], Baberi [17] and Malmir’s [6] studies. These studies showed that marital conflicts often result in verbal and physical conflicts, reduced social capital, and finally indifference and emotional divorce. Given the prominent role of domestic violence, characteristics of women who had demanded divorce due to violence have been examined. The variables of the others’ involvement, cultural differences, hatred and disdain, violence, and mental problems have significant relationships with emotional divorce.

In the studies of Jalili [2], Shakeri [10] and Kiaee Rad [29], it was emphasized that emotional divorce is one of the consequences of ongoing domestic violence, leading couples to continue living together for various reasons. Domestic violence often begins with neglect and verbal abuse, escalating to physical violence. These studies identified untreated psychiatric disorders, a lack of awareness among couples, and an inability to resolve marital issues as contributing factors to domestic violence. In Iranian society, the collectivist culture sometimes leads women who have experienced violence to feel ashamed to report it, resulting in stigma for being victims of abuse. Consequently, many do not seek help from counseling centers. One of the major challenges of psychological interventions for spousal abuse is that Iranian couples may avoid counseling. In fact, these women often prefer to cope with the emotional consequences of emotional divorce and domestic violence, enduring difficult situations rather than seeking counseling or filing for divorce. They believe that the repercussions of divorce in Iranian society are more burdensome than those of emotional divorce.Based on research findings, there is a significant and positive relationship between domestic violence and internet addiction among married women. Thus, increase in domestic violence is associated with an increase in internet addiction and vice versa. Using the internet reduces a person’s relationship with his/her family and makes the social circle around him/her small and increases feelings of loneliness and depression. Internet addiction causes family members to become away and an individual become irresponsible in doing her/his affairs also it disturbs the balance and structure of the family. All of them can provide the ground of domestic violence. Domestic violence is associated with many problems in victimized women. Such women have low self-consciousness, low cognitive confidence and negative beliefs. The findings of this study were consistent with other studies by Vameghi [28] and Johnson’s [26], whichshowed that factors such as loneliness, low self-consciousness and self-esteem, and negative beliefs lead individuals to further false excitement and out-of-family relationships, such as the internet and high use could be a precursor to internet addiction in people. In Movahhed’s study [18], it was found that internet addiction can lead individuals to experience heightened stress from consuming disturbing and threatening news and images online. This stress can permeate marital and family boundaries, resulting in negative interactions that contribute to marital and family conflicts and violence.

Limitations and future research

Limitations of this research include the lack of official, updated, and clear statistics on emotional divorce in Iran and specifically in Tehran. Additionally, while random sampling was used to select households for questionnaire completion, many participants were uncooperative. Some participants criticized the emotional divorce questionnaire for its binary options and lack of a broader spectrum. Data collection was based on self-reporting, and the sample size was relatively small.

Future research is needed to develop a local instrument for measuring emotional divorce among married women in Iran. Additionally, comparative studies in other cities in Iran should be conducted, and there is a need to design effective interventions aimed at reducing emotional divorce, internet addiction, and domestic violence among Iranian women.

Conclusions

The results of this research indicate that nearly half of married women in Tehran experience emotional divorce, internet addiction, and domestic violence. Given the detrimental effects of emotional divorce and its consequences on children—who are the future builders of society—preventing these damages can significantly contribute to the future mental health of the community.

To foster healthy and thriving families, and consequently a healthy society, it is essential for health system managers and planners to prioritize a preventive approach.
