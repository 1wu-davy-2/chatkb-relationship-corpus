ABSTRACT
To determine the relationship between subthreshold anxiety disorder and dry eye disease (DED) symptoms and signs. This was a cross-sectional study. The 127 outpatients of DED were collected and assessed for related symptoms and signs via the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI), strip meniscometry (SM), non-invasive breakup time (NIBUT), tear meniscus height (TMH), total corneal fluorescein staining (tCFS), and meibomian gland function (MGD) score. Subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders were evaluated via the HADS-A questionnaire and the Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview (M.I.N.I). The OSDI were markedly higher in patients with subthreshold anxiety relative to mental healthy patients (p = 0.029); however, these scores were lower than in the full-blown anxiety patients (p = 0.016). TMH and SM were substantially lower than those in the mental healthy patients (p = 0.002, p = 0.004, respectively) but higher than the full-blown anxiety cohort (p = 0.026, p = 0.029, respectively). HADS-A scores in the subthreshold, full-blown indicated a significant positive correlation with OSDI (r = 0.786, p < 0.001; r = 0.787, p < 0.001; respectively). Significant correlations were found between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED symptoms and signs; however, its intensity is lower than full-blown anxiety disorders.

BODY
Introduction

Dry eye disease (DED) is a chronic condition affecting the surface of the eye that results from various factors1. The incidence of DED has increased significantly with the advancement of society, the economy, and the persistent use of electronic devices such as smartphones, as well as due to the changes in lifestyle patterns. Its manifestations include dryness, grittiness, pain, and visual disturbances, which not only diminish the sufferer’s quality of life and work productivity but also adversely affect their psychological well-being2,3.

The literature suggests that DED sufferers are more prone to psychological conditions, such as anxiety and depression, especially the former disorder is more common and has an incidence of about 34.8%4. Furthermore, significant association has been observed between DED and anxiety disorders5–8. The considerable impact of various DEDs on patients’ quality of life and visual function has markedly heightened their psychological burden3 and prolonged symptoms can promote the development of psychological issues. Moreover, DED patients experiencing anxiety disorders have been found to have a higher prevalence of subjective symptoms than objective signs9,10. These subjective symptoms typically only show minimal improvements after DED treatment11, thus presenting considerable challenges for ophthalmologists and affected patients. Consequently, the association between DED and anxiety has emerged as a significant concern for both ophthalmologists and psychologists. In previous studies examining the correlation between DED and anxiety, anxiety was often defined as full-blown anxiety. Particularly in ophthalmology, due to a lack of psychological expertise, many ophthalmologists relied solely on self-rating anxiety questionnaires to diagnose anxiety. This method may inadvertently overlook a psychological condition that resembles full-blown anxiety disorder but does not fulfill its diagnostic criteria, referred to as subthreshold anxiety disorder12,13.

Subthreshold anxiety disorder tends to be more common than full-blown anxiety14 and under the prolonged influence of chronic diseases and other risk factors, it can progress to complete anxiety13,15. Associations between subthreshold anxiety disorder and various systemic diseases have been reported16–18. However, studies in the field of ophthalmology on the association between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED are still lacking. Further comprehensive research assessing the correlation between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED can provide benefits for the nuanced management and control of these interdisciplinary conditions. Therefore, this investigation aimed to evaluate the relationship between subthreshold anxiety disorder and the symptoms and signs of DED.

Results

Demographic and psychological characteristics of the study cohort

In this study, 127 DED patients were included and categorized into the following subgroups based on their psychological status: subthreshold anxiety disorder, full-blown anxiety disorders, and mental health group. The cohort consisted of 127 DED patients, divided into three subgroups based on psychological status: subthreshold anxiety disorder (n = 37; 16 men, 21 women), full-blown anxiety disorder (n = 18; 6 men, 12 women), and mental health (n = 72; 22 men, 50 women). The mean ages of the subthreshold, full-blown, and mental health groups were 27.3 ± 6.5, 30.4 ± 10.3, and 31.2 ± 9.6 years, respectively. The groups did not differ significantly in terms of age (p = 0.175) or gender distribution (χ2 = 1.754, p = 0.416).

Significant differences were identified in the incidence of insomnia among the three groups (χ2 = 23.94, p < 0.001). Specifically, the proportion of cases suffering from insomnia was significantly higher in both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups compared with the mental health group. Furthermore, the incidence of insomnia was greater in the full-blown anxiety disorder group than that in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group. In contrast, no significant differences were noted in the prevalence of refractive errors, habitual late-night activities, contact lens use, or excessive screen time across the three groups. Association of DED symptoms with anxiety severity

The data revealed marked differences in OSDI scores and duration of DED symptoms among the subthreshold anxiety disorder group, full-blown anxiety disorders group, and mental health group. The OSDI scores and duration of DED symptoms for both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups were significantly higher compared to the mental health group. Specifically, the OSDI scores for the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups compared to the mental health group were as follows: For the subthreshold anxiety disorder group, the OSDI was 42.08 (34.27–47.90) compared to 31.82 (20.11–45.71) in the mental health group (p = 0.029). For the full-blown anxiety disorder group, the OSDI was 65.84 (44.85–86.30) compared to 31.82 (20.11–45.71) in the mental health group (p < 0.000). Similarly, the duration of DED symptoms in these groups was significantly different. For the subthreshold anxiety disorder group, the duration of symptoms was 12.00 (9.00–24.00) compared to 9.00 (6.00–12.00) in the mental health group (p = 0.018). In the full-blown anxiety disorder group, the duration of DED symptoms was 36.00 (24.00–36.00) compared to 6.00 (6.00–12.00) in the mental health group (p < 0.000). Furthermore, there was a significant difference between the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups. The OSDI scores for the subthreshold anxiety disorder group were 65.84 (44.85–86.30), while for the full-blown anxiety disorder group, they were 42.08 (34.27–47.90), with a p-value of 0.016. The OSDI score for the subthreshold anxiety disorder group was 30.00 (24.00–36.00), while for the full-blown anxiety disorder group, it was 12.00 (9.00–24.00), with a p-value of 0.014. The proportion of individuals above the OSDI threshold was 77.8% in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group compared to 51.4% in the full-blown anxiety disorder group (p = 0.000). The duration of DED symptoms in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group was 30.00 (24.00–36.00) compared with 12.00 (9.00–24.00) in the full-blown anxiety disorder group (p = 0.014, Fig. 1A, B).

In addition, the subthreshold anxiety disorder group exhibited a strong positive correlation between the HADS-A and OSDI scores (r = 0.786, p < 0.000). Although a positive correlation between the HADS-A score and the duration of DED symptoms was identified (r = 0.163, p = 0.335), this association was not statistically significant. However, in the full-blown anxiety disorder group, significantly positive correlations were found between both the HADS-A and OSDI scores (r = 0.787, p < 0.001) and the duration of DED symptoms (r = 0.481, p = 0.044). Furthermore, when combining the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups, a significantly positive correlation was identified between the HADS-A score, OSDI scores (r = 0.796, p < 0.001), and the duration of DED symptoms (r = 0.418, p = 0.002).

The Mann-Whitney U test indicated significant differences in the OSDI scores and the duration of DED symptoms among the groups. The highest OSDI scores and duration of DED symptoms were recorded in the full-blown anxiety disorders, whereas the lowest scores and duration of DED symptoms were found in the mental health group. The subthreshold anxiety disorder group indicated intermediate levels of both metrics.

Association of DED signs with anxiety severity

The analysis of DED signs revealed significant differences in both TMH and SM values among the subthreshold anxiety disorder, full-blown anxiety disorder, and mental health groups. In comparison to the mental health group, both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups showed notably reduced TMH and SM values. Specifically, for TMH, the subthreshold anxiety disorder group had a value of 0.17 (0.20 − 0.15), while the mental health group had a value of 0.20 (0.15–0.23), with a p-value of 0.002. For the full-blown anxiety disorder group, TMH was 0.10 (0.10–0.14) compared to 0.20 (0.15–0.23) in the mental health group (p < 0.000). Similarly, for SM, the subthreshold anxiety disorder group had a value of 3 (2.5–4) compared to 4 (3–5) in the mental health group (p = 0.004). In the full-blown anxiety disorder group, SM was 2.05 (2–2.63) compared to 4 (3–5) in the mental health group (p < 0.000). Furthermore, the full-blown anxiety disorder group exhibited even lower TMH and SM values than the subthreshold anxiety disorder group. Specifically, for TMH, the full-blown anxiety disorder group had a value of 0.12 (0.10–0.14) compared to 0.17 (0.15–0.20) in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group (p = 0.029). For SM, the full-blown anxiety disorder group had a value of 2.05 (2–2.63), while the subthreshold anxiety disorder group had a value of 3 (2.5-4), with a p-value of 0.029 (Fig. 2A, B). Regarding correlations with the HADS-A score, no significant associations were identified between the HADS-A score and TMH, SM, NIBUT, tCFS, or MGD scores related to DED signs in both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups. However, when the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorder groups were combined, significantly negative correlations were identified between the HADS-A score and both TMH (r = − 0.357, p = 0.008) and SM (r = − 0.364, p = 0.006). No significant association was found between the HADS-A score and other parameters. Discussion

Subthreshold anxiety disorders are less severe than full-blown anxiety disorders; however, they have a higher prevalence and can also cause functional impairment13,15. This research study indicated that subthreshold anxiety disorder was more prevalent in patients with DED than full-blown anxiety disorders (29.1% vs. 14.2%). Although relevant ophthalmology literature for comparative analysis was lacking, these findings are consistent with previous non-ophthalmological studies14,18,19. It was also observed that full-blown anxiety disorders were less common among patients with DED than previously reported8. These data suggest that many subthreshold anxiety disorder patients may have been misclassified as having full-blown anxiety disorders in prior outpatient visits to ophthalmology clinics utilizing self-reported anxiety questionnaires. For instance, Crenier et al.20. revealed that for subthreshold anxiety disorder, the prevalence of overall anxiety issues was 26.2%, whereas it was 5.6% when only considering full-blown anxiety disorders. Therefore, ophthalmologists should be careful when assessing the psychological state of DED patients to prevent imposing additional psychological burdens.

The relationships between DED, psychological disorders, and sleep disturbances have garnered significant attention from researchers21–26. Here, the sleep patterns data of DED patients was analyzed, which revealed that the prevalence of self-reported insomnia reached 37.8% among all participants. Furthermore, in mental health DED patients, the rate of insomnia was 20.8%. These results highlight the correlation between DED and sleep quality, consistent with the findings of previous research27–30. After subgrouping all DED patients, the incidence of insomnia was found to be markedly greater in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group relative to the mental health group; however, it was lower than in the full-blown anxiety disorders group. The higher incidence of insomnia in the full-blown anxiety disorders group was within the hypothesized range, consistent with the prior literature indicating the high incidence of sleep disorders and their correlation with anxiety in anxious disorder populations31–34. The high incidence of insomnia in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group and reduced in the full-blown anxiety disorders group, was still significantly higher than the mental health group, suggesting that subthreshold anxiety disorder may also impact sleep. However, further investigations are needed to determine the mechanisms by which subthreshold anxiety disorder affects sleep and its association with DED.

The DED symptoms comparison revealed that the OSDI score and duration of symptoms with subthreshold anxiety disorder patients were markedly greater than those in the mental health and lower full-blown anxiety disorder groups. Furthermore, compared to the dry eye signs, TMH and SM were markedly less in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group than in the mental health group, but higher than the full-blown anxiety disorders group. These data suggest that subthreshold anxiety disorder is also closely related to DED, and this relationship primarily depends on DED symptoms and some parameters of physical signs. However, it was also inferred that the relationship between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED was not as strong as that with full-blown anxiety disorders. These results may be related to the pathological characteristics of subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders. Some researchers have suggested that subthreshold anxiety disorder may exhibit certain characteristics similar to full-blown anxiety disorders13,18, which could explain the parallels observed in the association between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED as reported in prior ophthalmology studies16,18. For instance, Sebastien Grenier et al.18. revealed that elderly patients with cardiovascular disease and subthreshold anxiety disorder had more similar signs and symptoms than those with full-blown anxiety disorders than differences. Subthreshold anxiety symptoms are not as severe as those seen in full-blown anxiety disorders; however, it impacts physiological functioning, the extent of which is not comparable to full-blown anxiety disorders. Overall, the characteristics of subthreshold anxiety disorder were found to be situated between those observed in the mental health and full-blown anxiety disorders groups, indicating a more pronounced difference from the mental health group.

Several domestic and international scholars have been investigating the associations between psychological disorders and DED symptoms and signs. Previous research indicates that psychological disorders, including anxiety and depression, are substantially related to DED symptoms; however, the association with DED signs remains relatively weak6–9,11. Kitazawa et al.6. performed a natural observation study that revealed significant correlations between anxiety and depression and DED symptoms. Furthermore, Mengliang Wu et al.21. revealed that anxiety and depression were associated with the severity of dry eye symptoms; however, no significant associations were observed with tear film breakup time (TBUT), corneal staining score, Schirmer I test, or MGD score. Moreover, some researchers have also evaluated anxiety and depression in DED patients over different age groups and indicated that both were markedly correlated with OSDI scores in all age cohorts35. The present study also found that the OSDI and HADS-A scores were positively associated in both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety groups. However, no marked correlation was observed between any DED signs and HADS-A scores. The combination analyses of the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders groups revealed that OSDI scores and the duration of DED symptoms had a significant positive correlation with HADS-A scores, while THM and SM indicated a significant negative correlation with HADS-A scores. The significant correlation between subthreshold anxiety disorder and DED symptoms observed in this study was anticipated; however, the finding that the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety group disorders had reduced TMH and SM compared to the mental health group was not hypothesized. This contrasts with most prior research, which has indicated a relatively weak correlation between psychological disorders such as anxiety and DED signs. However, a few studies have also yielded similar results. Mahmut Oğuz Ulusoy et al.7. analyzed tear parameters with the help of anterior segment OCT after diagnosing new patients with anxiety and depression and revealed that the tear parameters such as TMD, TMH, and TMA were notably reduced in the anxiety-depression group relative to the control group. Some researchers have proposed that the psychological state may further affect tear secretion by affecting the nervous system36. However, the results of this research study remain unclear due to statistical bias or a pathological mechanism.

This research study also compared correlations between DED symptoms and signs across all three groups and revealed that, except for the MGD score in the subthreshold anxiety disorder group, no DED signs in the subthreshold or full-blown anxiety disorders groups were significantly related to OSDI scores. Whereas all DED signs in the psychological health group indicated substantial correlations with OSDI scores. These results indicate that both subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders may influence the dissociation phenomenon between DED symptoms and signs, a research hotspot worldwide, when DED symptoms exceed corresponding clinical findings it poses substantial challenges for clinicians. The literature has indicated that psychological disorders such as anxiety are crucially linked with pronounced DED symptoms than signs37–39. This effect is likely attributable to the influence of psychological disorders on subjective DED symptoms through somatic manifestations9,40–44, thereby resulting in increasing DED symptoms than signs. Previous studies indicated that only full-blown anxiety disorder patients affect the association between DED symptoms and signs; however, here, it was demonstrated that subthreshold anxiety disorder may also impact the relationship between DED symptoms and signs. Furthermore, subthreshold anxiety disorder negatively affects pain-related disorders and these patients experience levels of suffering and social-psychological impairment comparable to those with full-blown anxiety disorders, both of which are markedly higher than non-anxiety individuals16. Moreover, even at subthreshold anxiety levels, physical symptoms can be observed and frequently serve as a key factor promoting DED symptoms over signs45. Therefore, the DED symptoms observed in the subthreshold anxiety disorder were not exclusively attributable to ocular manifestations but were potentially associated with somatic symptoms elicited by subthreshold anxiety disorder. However, the underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation.

Generally, traditional clinical studies have often utilized the non-anesthetic Schirmer test, which is inherently invasive. It has been found that individuals with anxiety have heightened ocular and psychological sensitivity, potentially adversely affecting their psychological state and test outcomes. Therefore, this research study employed the SM method as a replacement for the conventional non-anesthetic Schirmer test due to its rapid and non-invasive nature46 for better comfort of testing and the accuracy of results.

Although the OSDI questionnaire has been widely applied in previous DED studies, its integration into the present study provided new insights by distinguishing between subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders. This stratification revealed graded increases in symptom severity, suggesting that the extent of anxiety is a critical determinant of subjective disease burden in DED patients, a dimension not well characterized in earlier research. Anxiety patients often present with comorbid psychological conditions, such as depression; however, this study excluded cases with full-blown depressive disorder and did not assess subthreshold depressive disorder. Consequently, subthreshold depressive disorder may coexist among patients with subthreshold or full-blown anxiety disorders. Clinically, anxiety can be further classified into various types, each corresponding to distinct forms of subthreshold anxiety disorders; however, this study did not further stratify subthreshold anxiety disorder. In the demographic assessment, certain factors were based solely on self-reported yes/no responses, introducing a degree of subjectivity. Additionally, as this study did not include follow-up assessments, longitudinal data regarding the improvement of DED symptoms and the progression of subthreshold anxiety disorder throughout the treatment process were unavailable.

In conclusion, this study found that a significant proportion of DED patients exhibited subthreshold anxiety disorder, which is closely associated with both DED symptoms and specific clinical signs. Even at subthreshold levels, anxiety can exacerbate DED symptoms and contribute to pathological changes; however, the extent of this association is less remarkable compared to full-blown anxiety disorders. Consequently, ophthalmologists should conduct comprehensive anxiety assessments to avoid overestimating the severity of anxiety and ensure timely referrals for appropriate treatment. Such interventions may help improve both DED and subthreshold anxiety disorder simultaneously.

Methods

Study population

This cross-sectional investigation followed the Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Ophthalmology Hospital of Shandong First Medical University (SDSYEYY202212-1). All participants in the study.

completed written informed consent before they entered the study. The data were acquired from attendees of the dry eye clinic at Shandong First Medical University Ophthalmology Hospital between January 2023 and March 2024. The diagnostic criteria for DED were in accordance with the “Chinese expert consensus on the dry eye: definition and classification”47 and included: (1) Ocular surface disease index (OSDI) of ≥ 13 points, with a tear film breakup time (FBUT) of ≤ 5 s or a non-invasive breakup time (NIBUT) ranging between 10 and 12 s, or Schirmer I test results (in the absence of anesthesia) of ≤ 5 mm/5 minutes; (2) OSDI of ≥ 13 points, with FBUT > 5 s and ≤ 10 s, NIBUT between 10 and 12 s, or Schirmer I test results (in the absence of anesthesia) > 5 and ≤ 10 mm/5 minutes. (3) Concurrent with (2), corneal examinations showing corneal fluorescein sodium staining with positive staining at ≥ 5 staining points.

Patients were included in the investigation if they were aged ≥ 18 but < 65 years without gender restrictions, could understand and complete self-rating scales under physician guidance, and who had undergone psychological testing showing subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders or good psychological health.

Exclusion criteria included individuals who: were diagnosed with depression or other psychiatric disorders, had a history of oral psychotropic medication use, were diagnosed with ocular diseases deemed unsuitable for inclusion, had undergone prior surgical interventions, were suffering from DED secondary to systemic immune-related conditions, had active ocular diseases during their visit such as corneal epithelial defects, allergic conjunctivitis, acute bacterial conjunctivitis and uveitis, and who had anatomical abnormalities like incomplete eyelid closure, inversion, or eversion.

Moreover, DED patient’s demographic and lifestyle factors were comprehensively assessed including age, gender, refractive errors, insomnia, contact lenses, and excessive screen time. All information was obtained through subjective responses provided by patients during their consultations with ophthalmologists.

Diagnosis of anxiety disorders

All participants completed the HADS-A self-assessment questionnaire and the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (M.I.N.I). Subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders were diagnosed by: (1) A HADS-A score of ≥ 8 points48,49. (2) After M.I.N.I evaluation50,51, individuals not meeting all the criteria for the diagnosis of full-blown anxiety disorders and who had been excluded from psychological disorders such as depression, mental, neurological, and psychiatric disorders, were diagnosed with subthreshold anxiety disorder. (3) If an individual met al.l the diagnostic criteria for full-blown anxiety disorders and was excluded from the psychological disorders mentioned above, they were diagnosed with full-blown anxiety disorders.

Assessment of anxiety using the HADS-A scale

The HADS was employed by the subjects under the supervision of a physician. HADS is specifically designed for screening anxiety and depression in clinical patients. Zigmond and Snaith40 developed HADS, which has robust reliability and validity52. Therefore, it is specifically suitable for screening non-psychiatric outpatients in clinics53. HADS comprises 14 items with two subscales: A (anxiety) and D (depression), each including 7 items. The 4-point scoring system ranges from 0 to 3. Both subscales establish an 8-point cutoff value, with scores of ≥ 8 indicating diagnostic relevance. The HADS-A scale was utilized in this study for the evaluation of anxiety levels.

Diagnosis of anxiety disorders using the MINI

The M.I.N.I is a straightforward and reliable structured diagnostic questionnaire designed for mental disorders by DSM-IV and ICD-10 criteria. It has high reliability and validity, aligns with DSM-Ⅲ-R diagnoses, is user-friendly, and can be completed in approximately 15 min, making it highly suitable for implementation in clinical practice50. Therefore, this study employed the M.I.N.I to evaluate anxiety disorders, including agoraphobia, panic, generalized anxiety, social phobia, dysthymic anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment disorders. The aim was to diagnose subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders and exclude other psychiatric and neurological conditions. The interview was conducted by a senior psychiatrist.

Assessment of DED symptoms

The DED symptoms were primarily evaluated using the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) questionnaire54. This questionnaire was completed by the participants under the supervision of a single physician. The range of the OSDI score is between 0 and 100, with higher scores indicative of more severe symptoms. Furthermore, the duration of DED symptoms was incorporated as another evaluation criterion for DED symptoms.

Evaluation of DED signs

The primary clinical signs of DED include tear meniscus height (TMH), Non-invasive breakup time (NIBUT), Strip meniscometry (SM), total corneal fluorescein staining (tCFS) score, Meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) score.

All examinations were carried out by a masked ophthalmologist, with a 10-minute interval between each assessment to minimize potential interference. Furthermore, TMH and NIBUT were conducted via the Keratograph 5 M (Oculus Optikgeräte GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany)55. TMH was determined by assessing the average of 3 measured lowest points. The NIBUT was assessed through automatic detection of the initial break-up time; the patients were required to maintain a fixed stare without blinking. For each assessment, the average of the recorded 3 failure times was calculated. The SM employs the SMTube test strip under a slit lamp; patients were asked to position their heads on the chin rest, blink several times, then immerse the right side of the SMTube strip into the lower tear meniscus of their right eye for 5 s while holding it still. Subsequently, after removal of the strip, the length of the blue-soaked absorbent material was measured to record the SMTube score. Lastly, the patient’s left eye was assessed using the opposite end marked “L” on the tip46,56. Furthermore, the scoring system of the National Eye Institute (NEI) was employed to evaluate tCFS57. The MGD score was assessed by assessing the degree of blockage at the lower eyelid margin and the morphology of secreted meibomian lipids. The scoring ranged between 0 and 6 points, where the higher scores indicated poorer function58.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0 software. For data analysis, one eye was randomly selected from each patient. Shapiro-Wilk tests were used to assess data distributions. Continuous variables are shown as means with standard deviations (SD). Furthermore, the Mann-Whitney U test was carried out to compare parameter differences among the full-blown anxiety disorders, subthreshold anxiety disorders, and mental health groups. Spearman’s correlation coefficients were utilized to assess the relationships between anxiety scores and DED symptoms, elucidate the parameters in both the subthreshold and full-blown anxiety disorders, and analyze the correlations between DED symptom indicators and parameters across all three groups. A corrected p-value of < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.
