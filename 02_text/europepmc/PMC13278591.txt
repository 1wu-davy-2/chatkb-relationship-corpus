ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The prevalence of failure of Assisted Reproductive Techniques (ART) in women with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is high. One of the complications of the failure of these procedures is sexual problems. Counseling interventions may improve the sexual health of these women. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of solution-focused counseling on the sexual satisfaction and sexual quality of life (SQL) in women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This study is a randomized clinical trial carried out in women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART in the Reproductive Sciences Research Institute from March–August 2023 in Yazd, Iran. 60 samples were selected and randomized to intervention (8 solution-focused counseling sessions) and active control groups (educational booklets). The SQL and sexual satisfaction questionnaires were completed by both groups, before, immediately, and one month after the intervention. Data were analyzed by SPSS21.RESULTS:A statistically significant difference was reported between the sexual satisfaction scores in the two intervention and active control groups immediately (P < 0.01) and one month after the intervention (P < 0.001). Also, a significant difference was reported between the SQL scores in the two intervention and active control groups immediately (P < 0.05) and one month after the intervention (P < 0.02).CONCLUSIONS:Counseling based on the solution-focused approach increased sexual satisfaction and improved the SQL in women with PCOS with failure of ART. Therefore, it is recommended to use this approach in health and treatment centers to improve the sexual health of these women.

BODY
Introduction

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is the most common cause of infertility.[1] This disease has a significant global health burden that needs urgent attention and can affect women throughout their life, from adolescence to menopause.[2] About 75% of these women suffer from infertility due to anovulation.[3] The treatment of infertility in women with PCOS includes lifestyle changes, pharmacological therapies, surgical therapy or Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART).[456] The ART procedures include intrauterine sperm insemination, in vitro fertilization and intracytoplasmic sperm injection[7] and is widely used to help infertile couples with PCOS.[89] But only one-third of women who undergo ART procedures will achieve an intrauterine pregnancy, and a significant number of patients are subject to recurrent implantation failure (RIF).[10]

Although ART has improved outcomes for couples trying to fertility, RIF has created a new challenge. Repeated failed cycles can leave couples frustrated.[11]

Women with RIF are more likely to experience psychological distress such as depression (40.8%), anxiety (86%), stress (24%), marital problems, and decreased quality of life (70%), which can lead to sexual dysfunction.[101112] In addition, ovarian stimulation drugs used in the infertility treatment process, following implantation failure, cause hormonal changes and decrease hormones that affect sexual function in women.[10] Also, several studies have shown that pregnancy failure may lead to negative self-beliefs, concerns about sexual attractiveness, feelings of rejection, the fear of sex, sexual dysfunction, and decreased sexual satisfaction and sexual quality of life (SQL) in infertile women. The SQL is an image of the health status and general quality of life of people.[13141516] The stress caused by planned intercourse and the subsequent failure of fertility leads to a decrease in the desire and sexual satisfaction of these women.[17] Studies have recommended that the implementation of counseling programs by the health team can play an important role in promoting sexual health in women with failure of ART.[1418] Because sexuality is a major component of quality of life, counseling support may improve overall well-being and the chances of a long-term satisfying partnership.[15] Also, counseling and psychological interventions can increase the sexual satisfaction of infertile couples.[19] 80% of sexual problems can be solved if appropriate and adequate sexual health counseling is given.[20]

Some sexual counseling models have been investigated to promote the sexual health of couples and deal with the negative consequences of infertility, including ALARM, PLISSIT, Sexual Health Model, and PLEASURE. However, no definitive information has shown which model works best in improving sexual health in infertile women.[21] Another counseling intervention is the solution-focused approach, which has received less attention in previous studies.[22] Solution-focused counseling is a strengths-based, postmodern, and new approach in which the counselor focuses on clients’ strengths in life rather than their problems.[2324] In other approaches, the therapist pays more attention to the client’s past but in this approach, it pays attention to the present and the future and focuses on existing solutions in the Solution-focused approach, the therapist tries to understand how the client can manage his problems and recognize the behaviors that cause the problem to persist.[2526] However, a limited number of previous studies have assessed the effectiveness of solution-focused counseling in women with PCOS.

The prevalence of infertility among women with PCOS, the prevalence of psychological and sexual problems in these women following the failure of ART, such as decreased sexual satisfaction and SQL, and less attention to the solution-focused approach to improving the sexual health of these women in the past studies and the benefits of this approach was the factors that prompted us to conduct this study to determine the effectiveness of solution-focused counseling on the satisfaction and SQL in women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This parallel randomized clinical trial used a pretest-posttest design with an active control group. The study was conducted on 60 women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART referred to Reproductive Sciences Research Institute from March to August 2023in Yazd, Iran.

Study participants and sampling

The second author contacted eligible participants based on the list available at the center and the samples were randomly selected based on the inclusion criteria by reviewing the available records. Among the participants who had been referred to the Reproductive Sciences Research Institute affiliated with Shahid Sadoughi University of Medical Sciences, Yazd, Iran, the information of 85 women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART was checked. Finally, 60 participants who met the inclusion criteria entered the study [Figure 1].

Consort diagram

For randomization, the first-generation random allocation method was used and the eligible participants were allocated into two intervention and active control groups. A number from 1 to 60 was given to each participant. Then the participants were randomly assigned to two blocks of A and B using the website http://www.randomization.com. To comply with concealment, the selection of each block as an intervention or active control group was done by the fourth author based on a lottery.

Blinding was not possible in this study because the nature of the intervention was obvious.

It should be noted that this study adheres to CONSORT guidelines.

The number of 30 samples per group was determined based Kargar et al. study, using the G*power sample size formula, P = 5%, a test power of 80%, a confidence interval of 95%, and a subject attrition rate of 15%.[23]

Inclusion criteria were: willingness to participate in the study, Women with PCOS according to the Rotterdam criteria with a history of failure of ART, age between 18 and 45 years, no history of participating in sexual counseling sessions, cooperation in homework, and access to the internet and a smartphone. Exclusion criteria were: use of any drugs affecting sexual performance by the patient or her spouse, the presence of various types of severe mental disorders (psychosis, schizophrenia, etc.) under treatment, Presence of chronic diseases affecting sexual relations in the patient or her spouse such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

Data collection tool and technique

The main outcome was sexual satisfaction, which was recorded using Larson’s Sexual Satisfaction Questionnaire. The secondary outcome was SQL, which was recorded using an SQL questionnaire. The questionnaires were completed by the participants before the intervention, immediately after the intervention, and 1 month after the intervention. Larson’s Sexual Satisfaction Questionnaire: it was designed by Larson et al. in 1998. It includes 25 items and 4 components (desire to have sexual relations, sexual attitude, quality of sexual life, and sexual compatibility) and measures sexual satisfaction based on a 5-point Likert scale. The items include 13 negative and 12 positive items. The negative items are scored from never = score 5 to always = 1 and the positive items are rated from never = 1 to always = 5. The scale score range of this Questionnaire is 25–125.[27] This questionnaire has also been psychometrically validated in Iran. Moreover, the validity of this inventory has been evaluated by Bahrami et al. The reliability of this questionnaire has been reported a Cronbach’s alpha ˃0.7 for positive and negative items.[28] SQL questionnaire: It was designed and developed by Symond et al. (2005) to measure the SQL. It consists of 18 items and based on a 6-point Likert scale, scoring ranges from 6 to 1. The score range of this questionnaire will vary from 18 to 108. A higher score indicates a better level of SQL.[29] Also, Maasoumi et al. in 2013 was confirmed the validity and reliability of the Farsi version of this questionnaire for the first time in Iran.[30] Also, the demographic Characteristics Questionnaire included the woman’s age, spouse’s age, level of education of the woman and her spouse, woman’s occupation, spouse’s occupation, fertility information including a number of Reproductive techniques, and number of previous implantation failures.

Intervention

After obtaining informed consent, the participants were provided in two groups. At the beginning of the study, an online informed consent form was created and a link was sent to the women (https://digiform.ir/ca21c0b9a). The principle of respect for human dignity and freedom, providing sufficient information about objectives and methods of conducting research to the participants, gaining written informed consent, and respect for the principle of confidentiality of information were considered in this study.

For the intervention group (online group), 8 sessions (60 minutes) of solution-oriented counseling were held once a week on Saturdays from 16:00 to 17:00 in the Skyroom online environment. The online group formed two subgroups (each consisting of 15 women). At the end of each session, there was a question-and-answer session. In order to participate in counseling sessions, an SMS was sent to women the day before the session. The contents of each session are defined in Table 1.

An educational booklet was sent electronically in PDF format for the active control and intervention group. This booklet contained information on infertility and PCOS and its relationship with sexual factors with the following headings: Definition of infertility and its prevalence, causes, effective treatments PCOS, diagnosis, treatment, complications, the relationship between stress and infertility, ways to reduce stress, infertility and sexual problems, the effect of medical interventions on sexual relations, and strategies to improve sexual relations.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed in SPSS21 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to describe the data, and calculate the percentage, mean, and standard deviation. To compare the mean of the variables in each group inferential statistics. To compare qualitative variables used in the Chi-square test. Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to confirm the normality of data distribution. Due to the normality of the distribution of the quantitative variables in demographic form, the independent sample t-test was used to compare the variables in the two groups. Also, due to the normality of the distribution, repeated measures ANOVA test was used to compare the mean score of sexual satisfaction and SQL at three time points for each group. Further, due to the normality of data distribution, the Independent Sample T-test was used to compare the score of sexual satisfaction and SQL between the two groups at three points in time. In the data analysis, the confidence level of the tests was considered to be 95% (P < 0.05).

Discussion

The main goal of this study was to investigate the effectiveness of solution-focused counseling on sexual satisfaction and SQL in women with PCOS with a history of failure of ART. The results of the study showed that after the intervention, the sexual satisfaction and SQL scores significantly increased in the solution-focused counseling group and also, this increase was stable up to one month after the intervention.

The results of studies showed that women with PCOS with failed ART have sexual problems and, therefore, issues related to the sexual health of these women should be considered as an important component of comprehensive care.[313233] Also, Studies have recommended that the implementation of counseling interventions by the health team can play an important role in promoting sexual health in women with failed ART.[1418] Solution-focused counseling is a new and strengths-based approach in which the counselor focuses on the client’s strengths and may improve sexual health.[34]

The results of our study showed that solution-focused counseling increased the scores of sexual satisfaction in women with PCOS with failure of ART. In our review, we found limited studies on the effectiveness of this approach on women’s sexual health with PCOS with failure of ART. Therefore, some studies similar to the results of our research are reviewed. Karasu et al. investigated the effect of solution-focused counseling on marital dissatisfaction and sexual satisfaction in married people.[35] The results revealed a decrease in marital dissatisfaction and an increase in sexual satisfaction. Although this study was different from the current study in terms of the population studied, sample size, and inclusion and exclusion criteria, the results confirm the effectiveness of the solution-oriented approach on marital satisfaction and sexual satisfaction. Furthermore, in a clinical trial comparing the effect of solution-focused counseling and acceptance and commitment therapy on the marital satisfaction of housewives, Taghavi et al. (2020) showed that solution-focused therapy and acceptance and commitment-based therapy affect increasing psychological flexibility and marital satisfaction of housewives. However solution-focused treatment has been more effective in increasing marital satisfaction.[36] It seems that in the solution-focused approach, people by focusing on solutions can prioritize issues with a more constructive approach,[37] and this improves interpersonal relationships and increases individual satisfaction.[26]

Also, the results of our study showed that solution-focused counseling increases the SQL scores in women with PCOS with failure of ART. Bokaie et al. showed that SQL after online solution-focused counseling is improved among women with a history of breast cancer.[22] Although this study was different from the present study in terms of the studied population and sample size, the results of our research were similar to theirs.

Mohammadzadeh et al. showed that sexual counseling by the BETTER Model can improve the sexual function of infertile women.[21] The effect of counseling on sexual health was similar between our study and their study, but the number of counseling sessions and approaches were different. In another study, Kargar et al. concluded that eight 45-minute counseling sessions solution-focused group counseling can lead to increased sexual satisfaction of women with high BMI.[23] Although our study is similar to their study in terms of counseling approach and positive impact on sexual satisfaction, the research community differs. It seems that counseling with a solution-oriented approach by helping to solve individual and interpersonal problems increases the richness of the sexual relationship and as a result sexual satisfaction.[23]

The results of our study and similar studies show that counseling with a solution-focused approach can be used to improve satisfaction and SQL in different patient populations. The benefits of a solution-oriented approach, including not paying attention to past shortcomings and emphasizing individual abilities, have turned it into a practical intervention. The innovation of our study was the use of a solution-oriented approach to improve sexual satisfaction and SQL in women with PCOS with failure of ART. Considering the effectiveness of the solution-oriented approach, the researchers suggest that more studies be conducted in this field with more samples, more precise design, and a longer follow-up period.

Limitation and recommendation

One of the strengths of this research was that it was conducted at the Reproductive Sciences Research Institute, which is considered a scientific pole in Iran and a referral center. One of the limitations of this research was the annoyingness of talking about infertility and recalling bitter memories of the failure of ART results in a significant number of women with PCOS. Therefore, all participants were assured that the focus was on their abilities due to the use of a solution-oriented approach. Also, it was difficult for the participants to talk about sexual issues due to the cultural conditions of the studied society (Iran), and they were assured of their privacy and anonymity.

Conclusions

Counseling based on the solution-focused approach increased sexual satisfaction and improved the SQL in women with PCOS with failure of ART. Thus, considering the many sexual problems of infertile women with PCOS, improving the sexual health of these women increases their desire for fertility, and on the other hand, by improving the sexual health of these women, their quality of life increases, so, it is recommended to use this approach in health and treatment centers to improve the sexual health of these women.
