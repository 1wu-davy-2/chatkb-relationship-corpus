ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUND:Crises such as the coronavirus outbreak is associated with a lot of fear and anxiety. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between anxiety caused by coronavirus disease (COVID) and marital satisfaction mediated by empathy and conflict resolution styles in married women.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This descriptive-analytical study was carried out on 326 married women referring to a marriage counseling center in Tehran. Data were collected using the questionnaires of marital satisfaction, marital empathy, conflict resolution styles, and the coronavirus disease anxiety scale (CDAS). Data analysis was performed by Pearson correlation coefficient and structural equation modeling (SEM).RESULTS:The psychological symptoms of coronavirus disease anxiety (CDA) had a positive and significant effect on empathy and conflict resolution styles (P < 0.05) while the physical symptoms of CDA had a negative and significant effect on empathy, conflict resolution styles, and marital satisfaction (P < 0.05). The indirect effect of psychological symptoms of CDA on marital satisfaction due to empathy and conflict resolution styles was positive and significant (P < 0.05). The explained variance of marital satisfaction in terms of psychological and physical symptoms of CDA, empathy, and conflict resolution styles was equal to R2 = 0.42.CONCLUSION:Psychological symptoms of CDA increased marital satisfaction by affecting problem-solving styles and marital empathy. Therefore, empathy skills and conflict resolution styles can be used to reduce coronary anxiety and increase satisfaction levels.Keywords: Anxiety, conflict resolution, Covid-19, empathy styles, marital satisfaction, married women

BODY
Introduction

Marital satisfaction, as a sign of the quality of marriage, shows the understanding and contentment that spouses have with the satisfaction of their needs and wants by the other party.[1] It has a multidimensional and multipurpose concept that is composed of psychological, social, economic, and spiritual elements.[2] The rapid spread of the coronavirus around the world in 2020 has affected couples’ marital satisfaction by creating negative emotions.[3] Research showed that couples who are affected by negative emotions caused by coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) quickly become upset and feel out of control and powerless to make decisions so that the marital relationship leads to multiple conflicts.[4]

In addition to physical symptoms including fever, cough, and respiratory problems, the coronavirus disease (COVID) is associated with psychological symptoms, including anxiety, which is a common symptom in patients with chronic respiratory disorders.[5,6] The research shows that coronavirus disease anxiety (CDA) is due to the unknown and ambiguous nature of this disease, as well as little scientific information about it, which leads to the exacerbation of anxiety.[7] Hamouche[8] argues that during the outbreak of COVID and quarantine, when schools, universities, and many businesses were closed and people were more likely to leave their homes in emergencies, unique conditions have arisen that have uncertain short-term and medium-term psychological consequences. Moreover, researchers believe that crises such as the coronavirus disease/COVID outbreak, which are always associated with increased fear and anxiety, significantly impair daily functioning and threaten mental health.[9]

According to previous studies, women were more likely than men exposed to stress and mental health problems during COVID-19. These studies reported greater psychological impact and higher levels of stress, anxiety, and depression in the early stages of COVID in women.[10] The increasing prevalence of COVID in Iran has also increased the risk of anxiety and stress among families.[11] Psychological traumas can have negative effects on the quality of marriage, which is one of the prerequisites for family health.[12]

Studies show that empathy can moderate anxiety and inappropriate emotional reactions.[13] Researchers believe that empathy, along with other elements of communication such as the roles and value system of couples, is a fundamental part of the structure of the marital relationship.[14] Empathic people have positive feelings about the people around them, build constructive relationships with others, have effective verbal interactions, and are sensitive to non-verbal interactions.[15]

In addition to empathy, conflict resolution styles are also among the concepts that can play a significant role in the quality of marital life and marital satisfaction of women.[16] Rahim considers conflict as an interactive state that is manifested in oppositions, differences, and incompatibilities between individuals and groups. There are several sources of conflict in married life, including differences between spouses in their system of values, needs, and expectations.[17] Findings show that spouses try to resolve their conflicts in a variety of ways, from shouting at each other to finding a solution that is acceptable to both parties.[18] Guttman argues that when conflicts arise, the style that couples use to resolve them reflects the level of satisfaction they experience in their relationships.[19] Marital satisfaction is closely related to couples’ conflict resolution styles and conflict resolution patterns are one of the strong predictors of marital satisfaction.[20] Rahim[21] to resolve the conflict, has considered five approaches including dominant, mandatory, avoidant, conciliatory, and integrative styles. In the case of the integrative style, which is the same as the problem-solving style, people are looking for a solution that is approved by both sides. This style is known as the win-win style and researchers consider it the most appropriate and effective style for solving problems.[22]

Since women are more prone to stress and mental health problems during COVID-19 than men, they are more vulnerable and experience more anxiety.[10] On the other hand, ensuring women's mental health guarantees family health and consequently community health.[23] Therefore, the present study investigated the relationship between anxiety caused by COVID and marital satisfaction mediated by empathy and conflict resolution styles in married women.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

The present study is a descriptive-analytical type that investigates causal relationships through a correlation between variables.

Study participants and sampling

The study population consisted of married women referring to the counseling center and psychological services of Hamkadeh in Tehran. For many women, a research project link was sent and after providing explanations, they were asked to help us in this research if they wished. According to offered sample size guideline by Kline for analyzing structural equation models,[24] while taking into account the possibility of attrition, 326 people were determined as samples, which has been used in Esmailian et al. study.[25] People were selected by convenience sampling method from among the studied population.

The criteria for entering the study are not suffering from physical and mental diseases and drug addiction in a way that affects the results of the study, and the exclusion criteria included participation in simultaneous intervention studies and unexpected pregnancy and surgery.

Data collection tool and technique

In this study, after coordinating with the management of Hamkadeh Psychological Counseling and Services Center, the objectives of the study were explained to the participants and then their verbal consent was obtained to complete the questionnaires. Participation in the study was completely voluntary and people were assured that their information is confidential. Questionnaires were given to people and they answered simultaneously. The questionnaires included marital satisfaction, marital empathy, conflict resolution styles, and the coronavirus disease anxiety scale (CDAS).

The Enrich marital satisfaction scale was used to assess marital satisfaction. This scale was designed by Fowers and Olson (1989) and has two forms of 115 questions and 125 questions, which consists of 12 subtests.[26] Soleimanian has designed the short form of scale in 47 questions and reported the reliability of it as 0.65 through Cronbach's alpha.[27] The concurrent validity of this questionnaire based on its main form was 0.95.[26] The answer to questions is on a 5-point Likert scale from 1 completely agree to 5 completely disagree, and finally, the scores are collected together. Scores less than 30 indicate severe dissatisfaction of spouses from marital relationships, 30 to 40 as dissatisfaction, 40 to 60 moderate satisfaction, 60 to 70 high satisfaction, and scores above 70 indicate extraordinary satisfaction.

The basic empathy scale of Jolliffe and Farrington (2006) was used to measure marital empathy. This scale has 20 questions and included two subscales of excited-affectionate and cognitive. This questionnaire is an attitude-measuring tool of a 5-point Likert scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Its minimum and maximum scores are 20 and 100, respectively. A low score indicates a lack of marital empathy and a high score indicates a high level of marital empathy. Albiero et al.[28] used construct validity to determine the validity of the test and obtained the approved results. Moreover, Jafari et al.[29] validated the Persian form of the scale and its reliability was reported between 0.74 and 0.80 by Cronbach's alpha method.

Rahim Organizational Conflict Inventory-II (II-ROCI) (1983) is used to measure conflict resolution styles. This questionnaire has 28 items and five subscales including integrated style, dominant style, mandatory style, avoidant style, and compromising style. The items on this scale are scored on a 5-point Likert from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). A higher score on each subscale indicates a person's preference for using that subscale.[21] Cronbach's alpha coefficient of this inventory was calculated at 0.79 for the whole scale, 0.73 for the constructive conflict resolution style subscale, and 0.70 for the non-constructive conflict resolution style subscale.[30]

The CDAS is for measuring anxiety caused by the outbreak of coronavirus, which has been prepared and validated in Iran. CDAS has 18 items and 2 psychological and physical components. This scale is scored on a 4-point Likert scale from “never” to “always” and its score range is from 0 to 54. A high score on this scale indicates a higher level of anxiety. Moreover, its reliability was obtained using Cronbach's alpha method for psychological and physical symptoms, and the whole questionnaire 0.879, 0.861, and 0.919, respectively.[6]

Ethical consideration

In this study, the principles of ethics were observed during the research process. In addition, the present study was approved by Torbat Heydarieh University of Medical Sciences with the ethical code No. IR.THUMS.REC.1400.002.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed by the Pearson correlation coefficient and the structural equation modeling (SEM), as well as with the IBM SPSS Statistics 27and IBM SPSS Amos 24 software.

Before the statistical analysis, the data were screened and univariate outliers were identified with a box diagram. The results revealed that there is no univariate outlier. However, Mahalanobis statistics showed that there are two cases of multivariate outliers. Therefore, these people were removed and the analysis continued with the data of 324 people. In examining the assumptions, the results of the Kolmogorov–Simonov test also showed that the standardized residual values are normal (P < 0.05, df = 324, Z = 0.04). The ratio of the multivariate kurtosis indicator (4.14) to the critical value (2.41) was also equal to 1.72. Finally, the assumption of error independence was fulfilled.

The research's conceptual model was investigated after confirming the assumptions. The model was estimated by the maximum likelihood method. Investigating path coefficients in the initial analysis revealed no significant relationship between the psychological symptoms of coronary anxiety and marital satisfaction (P < 0.05). Moreover, model fit indices were not at the desired level [Table 2]. Accordingly, based on Meyers et al.,[31] the model was modified through eliminating the insignificant paths and establishing the covariance of the observed variables. Table 2 shows the better fit of the modified model. Path coefficients in Figure 1 exhibit the higher factor load of the emotional dimension compared to the cognitive dimension in the empathy measurement model. Furthermore, in the model of conflict resolution styles, the most factor load is related to the required conflict resolution style (all factor loads were P < 0.05). Based on the results, the psychological symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety had a positive and significant effect on empathy and conflict resolution styles (P < 0.05). Nevertheless, the physical symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety negatively and significantly affect empathy, conflict resolution styles, and marital satisfaction (P < 0.05). Besides, empathy and conflict resolution styles directly, positively, and significantly affected marital satisfaction (P < 0.05).

Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the relationship between COVID-19 anxiety, empathy, and conflict resolution styles with the marital satisfaction of married women. The results revealed that the effect of psychological symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety on empathy is positive and significant, while the physical symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety on empathy were negative and significant. The positive and significant relationship between COVID-19 anxiety and empathy is consistent with the results of Knight et al.[32] They believed that as empathy is the ability to understand the emotional experience of others, it is closely related to compassion and attention to others, so this increase in emotional awareness and sensitivity may be associated with increased anxiety. On the other hand, higher anxiety may be considered to increase concern for others and can be associated with increased empathy.[32] The negative and significant association of physical symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety such as tremors, palpitations, and headache with empathy may be due to the unfavorable physical condition that the person is involved in at the moment and reduces the quality of interpersonal relationships and focuses more on physical symptoms of anxiety. Moreover, the psychological symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety had a positive and significant effect on conflict resolution styles. This finding was consistent with the study of Hashemi and Rashidi.[33]

Investigations showed that most studies found an inverse and significant relationship between corona anxiety and marital satisfaction. Therefore, a study with inconsistent results with the present study was not found. Some similar studies showed that there is no significant relationship between anxiety caused by death and quality of life.[34] Explaining this result, it should be noted that high anxiety in marital relationships increases marital conflicts and upsets the balance in cohabitation, and it causes couples to try different methods for solving conflict problems. Therefore, with the increase in anxiety, the use of conflict resolution styles increases. However, the physical symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety had a significant negative effect on conflict resolution styles. As a result, it appears to cause a decline in performance in interpersonal relationships.

Other results showed that there is a positive and significant relationship between empathy and marital satisfaction. This finding was in line with the study of Montazer Soomeh-Sarai and Asadi Mojreh.[14] Empathy, along with other elements of a relationship, forms a fundamental part of the structure of the marital relationship and reduces discomfort, and increases the sense of security in the marital relationship.[35] Empathy is one of the essential skills for successful interpersonal relationships[36] and makes couples have a caring attitude towards each other and believe in each other resulting in reduced interpersonal conflicts and improved marital relationships and ultimately marital satisfaction.[37]

Here, conflict resolution styles also had a positive and significant relationship with marital satisfaction. The study of Azadifard and Amani also confirms this result.[38] Explaining this finding, it can be said that an integrated conflict resolution method helps couples to have a positive attitude towards each other. Furthermore, people who use a compromising approach usually pay attention to the expectations and needs between themselves and others and pay attention to the distribution of points between the parties. Steuber also believes that the way to avoid conflict actually involves ignoring the spouse, not cooperating, and not being accountable.[19]

The overall results of this study showed that the psychological symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety indirectly explain a significant part of marital satisfaction through the mediation of conflict resolution styles and marital empathy and increase the level of marital satisfaction. So far, no direct study has been found to reach this conclusion. It is clear from the results of the study that the increase in the psychological symptoms of COVID-19 anxiety causes an increase in the level of marital empathy and the use of integrated conflict resolution styles. Then, by increasing them, the level of marital satisfaction increases. Hashemi and Rashidi[33] found that COVID-19 anxiety explains a significant percentage of changes in marital boredom due to marital conflict resolution styles and distress tolerance.

This study had limitations, such as the fact that due to the corona situation, questionnaires were completed online, which can have any bias, so attention should be paid to generalizing its results.

Conclusion

Based on the results, the psychological symptoms of CDA can increase marital satisfaction by affecting problem-solving styles and marital empathy; therefore, it is suggested to use empathy skills and conflict resolution styles to reduce coronary anxiety and increase the level of satisfaction.
