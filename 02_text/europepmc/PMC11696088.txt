ABSTRACT
Abortion can be a very stressful experience for women, and many social and psychological factors can influence this stress. The present study aims to investigate the impact of perceived social support on the marital satisfaction of women who have undergone an abortion. The study also explored whether resilience played a mediating role in this relationship. Data were analyzed from the cross-sectional study. The Perceived Social Support (including Social support, friend support, and other support subscales), ENRICH marital satisfaction, and Connor-Davidson Resilience questionnaire were completed by 150 women who had undergone an abortion and were referred to hospitals and clinics of Jahrom University of Medical Sciences from April 2021 to June 2021. The structural equation modeling (SEM) approach was used to obtain the direct and indirect effects of Perceived Social Support subscales and Resilience on marital satisfaction. The study found that the women who participated had high marital satisfaction and social support scores, while their resilience scores were moderate. The results indicated a positive correlation between social support dimensions and marital satisfaction and resilience. The study also revealed that family support (β = 0.41, p < 0.001), other supports (β = 0.46, p < 0.001), and resilience as a mediating variable (β = 0.15, p < 0.001) had a direct and significant positive impact on marital satisfaction. However, none of the three domains of perceived social support showed any indirect significant relationship with marital satisfaction. The study’s findings demonstrate that the perception of social support from family members and other individuals can significantly impact the level of satisfaction in a marriage. Appropriate social support can increase resilience and help couples better cope with the stress associated with abortion. This study suggests that educating families, healthcare providers, and other individuals can help women experiencing this issue by providing them with counseling and support. This can ultimately help women maintain their mental and sexual health.

BODY
Introduction

One of the challenges women face during their reproductive years is abortion, particularly after a wanted pregnancy. This can lead to physical consequences such as bleeding or infection, as well as mental health issues, including increased risks of anxiety, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder, and even suicide1. However, some studies suggest that abortion due to unwanted pregnancies does not significantly affect women’s mental health2. In Iran, the fetus is regarded as sacred, and abortion is permitted only for medical reasons or if it occurs spontaneously3. Additionally, within the Iranian sociocultural context, a woman’s reproductive health is often closely tied to the overall health of her family. For many couples, the inability to conceive can lead to a devastating emotional experience, particularly for women, resulting in numerous negative outcomes. For many Iranian women, motherhood is a crucial aspect of their identity, and their biological, psychological, and social well-being can depend on their ability to have children4. When women are unable to conceive, they may feel inadequate, which can significantly impact their quality of life and relationships, sometimes leading to difficulties in sexual intercourse and diminished marital satisfaction5. Marital satisfaction arises from the compatibility and satisfaction between husband and wife in various aspects of their shared life. It is also a crucial factor in managing mental stress and performing well in life6.

A study conducted on German couples revealed that abortion can cause stress, which can lead to dissatisfaction and even termination of marital relationships. Conversely, marital dissatisfaction can affect the decision to have an abortion7. Factors that affect marital satisfaction include empathy, expression of interest and intimacy, understanding of feelings and respect for the spouse, and perceived social support8. Perceived social support refers to the level of love, companionship, care, respect, attention, and help received by an individual from other individuals or groups such as family members, friends, etc9.

Research has shown that couples with low social support have lower marital satisfaction, and low social support has been associated with abortion10. Tseng et al. investigated couples who experienced a decline in their marital satisfaction following abortion-related grief, insufficient family support, and lack of financial and emotional empathy. However, if various supports from others and effective psychological interventions are applied after abortion, women’s resilience will be improved, which can in turn reduce the negative psychological effects of abortion on married life11.

Resilience is a dynamic process that involves positive adaptation in the face of significant adversity. This concept includes two key components: exposure to serious threats or challenges; and the ability to achieve positive adaptation despite these significant challenges12. Resilience leads to improved coping skills, higher self-efficacy, and reduced perception of stress13. It empowers individuals to handle future problems, enhances their capacity to withstand pressures, and increases their ability to adapt and overcome difficulties and hardships14. Some studies suggest that resilient individuals tend to have higher levels of family cohesion, improved communication, and better family functioning15. Perceived social support is also a significant factor in coping with, adapting to, and building resilience in stressful situations, greatly affecting people’s physical and mental well-being, life satisfaction, and marital status16. The support received from family, friends, and others serves as a crucial predictor of resilience; generally, the greater the amount of support, the higher the level of resilience17. Supportive relationships offer a strong foundation of resilience, as those who provide social support often demonstrate intimacy, care, and attention and can recognize individual strengths18. Resilient individuals tend to remain emotionally calm, approach problems with creativity, and develop plans for a solution. They also know when to seek help from others19. Ultimately, these traits contribute to their mental health and help them maintain marital satisfaction through resilience20.

A study conducted in New York found that resilience plays a significant role in couples’ marital satisfaction. This is achieved by creating a relationship between a set of attachments and spouse support21. Another study by Biggs et al. revealed that women who had undergone abortions and experienced sexual and physical abuse had lower resilience and marital satisfaction22. Meanwhile, research by Shaykholeslami et al. in Iran showed that non-resilience is a critical factor that contributes to incompatibility in relationships. It can lead to low self-confidence and a lack of peace, especially during difficult times. In general, resilience refers to the process of dealing with incompatibility in relationships, change, or opportunity in a way that helps identify, strengthen, and enrich resistance factors. This, in turn, provides self-protection against negative and stressful factors. Therefore, resilience helps maintain and promote an individual’s mental health and that of their loved ones by moderating the psychological stress caused by problems23,24.In recent years, the incidence rates of abortions have been increasing in Iran, with 9 out of 1000 women undergoing the procedure. At the same time, there has been an emphasis on increasing births as part of government policy4. It is important to consider the possible consequences of abortion on couples, including psychological, social, and sexual problems, and impairments in the quality of marital relationships. Some studies suggest that social and psychological factors such as social support and resilience may improve satisfaction with life, particularly marital satisfaction. Considering the sociocultural context of this study and the emphasis on preventing abortion, as well as the significance placed on the fetus and strong family support, there have been no studies exploring the relationship between perceived social support, marital satisfaction, and resilience in women who have undergone an abortion. Therefore, this study aims to examine the effect of perceived social support on marital satisfaction among women who have experienced an abortion, while also considering the mediating role of resilience.

Methods

Design and sample

This is a descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study that was carried out using structural equation modeling, with 150 women who had experienced abortion selected from hospitals and clinics of Jahrom University of Medical Sciences from April to June 2021. According to Kline25, the sample size was determined using the rule of thumb of 10–20 cases per parameter for stable parameter estimation in Structural equation modeling (SEM), with inclusion criteria such as being Iranian, aged between 17 and 44 years, having literacy, having no fetal death in the third trimester of their previous pregnancy, and experienced abortion in the past week to a year. After obtaining permission from the university ethics committee (IR.SUMS/REC 19909) and written informed consent from the participants, the researcher provided them with a questionnaire and brief explanations about the research objectives. 95% of the eligible individuals offered the opportunity to participate in this study agreed to take part. It should be noted that all methods were performed under the relevant guidelines and regulations of the Shiraz University of Medical Sciences (SUMS).

Instruments and measures

Data was collected using four questionnaires: the demographic questionnaire, the multidimensional scale of Perceived Social Support, the Marital Satisfaction scale, and the Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC).

Demographic questionnaire

The demographic questionnaire consisted of questions about age at abortion, the complete history of midwifery, contraception method used before pregnancy, number of children, level of education, marital status, employment status, method of contraception, and reasons for abortion.

Perceived social support scale

The multidimensional scale of Perceived Social Support (Zimet et al., 1988) was comprised of 12 items and included three areas of social support received from family (4 items), social support received from friends (4 items), and social support received from relatives (4 items). This questionnaire was scored based on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagreeing = 1 to strongly agree = 5, with a possible score range of 12 to 60. Previous research has demonstrated acceptable validity and reliability for the Persian version of this questionnaire26. In the current study, the instrument showed strong reliability, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.92.

ENRICH marital satisfaction scale

The ENRICH Marital Satisfaction Scale, which consisted of 47 questions and nine domains, measured satisfaction with various aspects of marriage. The questionnaire components included personality issues, marital relationships, conflict resolution, financial management, leisure activities, sex, marriage and children, relatives and friends, and religious orientation. This questionnaire was answered based on a five-point Likert Scale, ranging from strongly agree = 1 to strongly disagree = 5, with some questions scored in reverse. The possible score range was 47 to 225, with scores above and below 152 indicating high and low satisfaction, respectively27. The Persian version of this questionnaire has shown acceptable validity and reliability in prior research28. In the present study, the instrument demonstrated good reliability, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.85.

The Connor-Davidson resilience scale

The Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC) includes 25 items, divided into five components: perception of individual competence (8 items), trust in individual instincts and negative emotion tolerance (7 items), positive acceptance of change and secure relationships (5 items), control (3 items), and spiritual impact (2 items). Responses are rated on a six-point Likert scale ranging from zero (completely incorrect) to five (always correct), with possible scores ranging from 0 to 100. Higher scores indicate greater resilience29. The CD-RISC was standardized in Iran and found to be reliable30. In this study, the instrument demonstrated acceptable reliability, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.80.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis included sociodemographic characteristics of mothers. The Pearson correlation test of the study variables was also performed as a baseline analysis. Before developing the statistical model, key assumptions were carefully assessed, including normality, linearity, and the presence of outliers. No considerable violations were found that would impact the validity of the results. The (SEM) was used to investigate the mediation of resilience, the degree to which intermediate variables in a putative causal chain transmit the effect of support on marital satisfaction. The mediational modeling permitted estimates of the indirect effect of family support, friends support, and other observable support on marital satisfaction via resilience. Analyses estimated the total effect of support on marital satisfaction, partitioning this effect into the direct contribution of perceived social support on marital satisfaction and the indirect effect of this relationship resilience. Interpretation of the parameter estimates of the SEM model follows the same logic as the regression coefficients, the one-point increase in the response variable is associated with the estimated change in the predictor.

To investigate the mediation of resilience, the SEM model was implemented via AMOS software version 24.0, which was based on the maximum likelihood method as the estimation procedure. The strength of fit of the SEM model was investigated based on multiple indices, including the root-mean-square error of approximation (RMSEA), comparative fit index (CFI), Tucker-Lewis index (TLI), and the Normed Fit Index (NFI). The following cut-off values, which were suggested by Hu and Bentler [13], indicate a good fit: RMSEA 0.95 TLI > 0.95, and NFI > 0.9. Other analysis was also performed using the software package SPSS version 25 and P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant correlation.

Discussion

The present study aimed to investigate the effect of perceived social support on marital satisfaction of women who have experienced abortion considering the mediating role of resilience in patients referred to hospitals and clinics of Jahrom University of Medical Sciences. The final structural model revealed that the resilience variable, along with family support and other support, had a significant impact on marital satisfaction. The results of this study also align with those of Qadir et al. (2013), who highlighted the positive role of social support in improving marital satisfaction and mental health in Pakistani women. Both studies emphasize the critical influence of family as a primary source of support in culturally conservative contexts31. However, while they found that social support indirectly enhances marital satisfaction through improved relationship dynamics, this study did not identify significant indirect effects of social support via resilience. Additionally, the focus on resilience as a distinct mediator in this study provides a unique contribution compared to Qadir et al., who did not examine resilience separately. Together, these findings highlight both shared and unique pathways through which social support contributes to marital satisfaction, emphasizing the need for culturally sensitive approaches to address the psychological effects of abortion.

The findings of some studies showed that women with better social support from a sexual partner, friend, or family member, had better psychological regulation and reported a higher quality of life and marital satisfaction after participating in post-abortion group discussions and sharing their experiences with others, as well as enjoying their support. They indicated that social support, especially family, plays the most important role in predicting marital satisfaction32–34. In contrast to the findings of this study, Kiani et al. did not find a positive and direct relationship between family support and the resilience of individuals who experienced grief. Instead, they observed that support from friends and relatives had a positive impact on resilience35. Similarly, Shih et al.36 reported that some families can worsen anxiety in patients and provide inadequate support during stressful situations. This result can be due to the fact that women who experience abortion, particularly multiple abortions, may suffer from various psychological stresses such as impulsive behaviors, anger, anxiety, feelings of helplessness, and worthlessness, leading to sexual dissatisfaction and even termination of intercourse. This emotional stress can reduce an individual’s psychological well-being as it drains their psychological resources. Social support providers express intimacy, provide the necessary emotional care and attention, and help people deal with stress by emphasizing their abilities. The greater the depth of social support, including support from family, friends, and relatives, the stronger people will be in the face of problems and the less vulnerable they will be. Secure support, trust, empathy, proper care, and higher resilience are essential components of satisfactory relationships that lead to life satisfaction, especially marital satisfaction.

The present study demonstrated a significant relationship between resilience and marital satisfaction; however, social support has a stronger impact on marital satisfaction and does not require a mediating variable. Consequently, none of the three areas of perceived social support showed a significant indirect effect on marital satisfaction through resilience. In line with this study, Shaykholeslami23 found a positive correlation between resilience and perceived social support concerning mothers’ adjustment. They concluded that perceived social support has a more substantial effect on predicting mothers’ adjustment than resilience. In contrast, some research highlighted the essential role of social support in predicting resilience and enhancing happiness. Furthermore, resilience is a critical factor that aids individuals in adapting to grief and coping with child loss37–39. Some researchers suggest that having an abortion can lead to stress, dissatisfaction, and even the deterioration of marital relationships. However, individual resilience can positively influence marital satisfaction7,21. Social support plays a crucial role in enhancing resilience and alleviating psychological distress by fostering a sense of worth and respect in individuals39, It appears that support from family, close friends, and healthcare professionals during challenging times can contribute to greater marital satisfaction and reduce negative psychological consequences. This support helps individuals better understand stressful events and minimizes the adverse effects of difficult experiences such as abortion. In the context of this study, which focuses on Iranian sociocultural factors, the significance of fetus preservation in planned pregnancies, along with strong emotional connections within families and supportive friendships, is noteworthy.

Implication

The study’s findings emphasize the importance of fostering supportive environments to enhance marital satisfaction and resilience among women who have experienced abortion, particularly in culturally sensitive contexts like Iran. Practical applications include training healthcare providers and counselors in culturally tailored approaches, implementing community-based support programs, and leveraging social media for awareness campaigns to educate families and reduce stigma. By addressing cultural norms and creating accessible support systems, these interventions can help mitigate the psychological and relational impacts of abortion, ultimately improving women’s quality of life and mental health.

Limitation

This study has also several limitations that should be considered when interpreting its results. First, the majority of participants were women who had undergone a single abortion, which limits the generalizability of the findings to women with multiple or recurrent abortion experiences. Second, the study employed a cross-sectional design, which prevents the establishment of causal relationships between perceived social support, resilience, and marital satisfaction. Longitudinal research is needed to examine how these relationships develop over time and assess the sustained impact of these factors. Another limitation concerns the sampling method and scope of the study. Data were collected from a single-center population in hospitals and clinics associated with Jahrom University of Medical Sciences, which may limit the diversity of participants in terms of socioeconomic, cultural, and geographic backgrounds. Future studies should aim to include a broader and more representative sample across multiple centers to enhance generalizability. Methodologically, self-reported questionnaires were used to assess social support, marital satisfaction, and resilience. While widely accepted, these tools are susceptible to biases, such as social desirability or recall bias, which may affect the accuracy of the results. Additionally, the study did not control for other potentially confounding variables, such as the participants’ mental health history, economic status, or level of education, which may have influenced the outcomes. Further research addressing these limitations could provide a more robust understanding of these relationships.

Future research directions

Future studies should explore the dynamics of perceived social support, resilience, and marital satisfaction across diverse cultural settings to identify universal and culture-specific patterns. Longitudinal studies are needed to understand how these relationships evolve, while investigations into additional variables, such as religious beliefs, stigma, and economic challenges, could provide a more comprehensive perspective. Intervention-based studies should focus on designing and evaluating culturally sensitive programs, such as family counseling or resilience-building workshops, to establish best practices for supporting women post-abortion.

Conclusion

Abortion can be a significant event in a person’s life. In some contexts, experiencing an abortion after an unintended pregnancy may have minimal psychological consequences. However, in the Iranian context, it can lead to long-term effects on the mental health of couples. These effects may ultimately decrease their quality of life and marital satisfaction. On the positive side, appropriate social support and increased resilience can help couples cope more effectively with the stress associated with abortion. This support can come from various sources, including a spouse, family members, friends, counselors, or healthcare providers. By offering proper training, emotional support, and utilizing social media, it is possible to reduce the psychological and sexual consequences of abortion, thereby improving the quality of life for women and their families.
