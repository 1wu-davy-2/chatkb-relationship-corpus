BODY
PROBLEM

First‐year dental hygiene students practice instrumentation 3–6 hours a week for 12 weeks using manikins and a student partner. We observed that the students, most of whom are generation Z, quickly became bored of practicing the same instruments repeatedly on a single classmate for 90 minutes at a time, which aligns with evidence that this generation has short attention spans. 1 Therefore, we developed instrumentation speed dating, an engaging, fast‐paced activity to maintain student engagement in developing mastery of instrumentation.

SOLUTION

Instrumentation speed dating is a station rotation activity during which students take on the roles of clinicians and patients. Student clinicians have 8 minutes to practice a specific instrument on a designated area of the mouth of each of eight student patients during the first half of a 180‐minutes session, with student patients acting as clinicians for the remaining 90 minutes. Interacting with multiple student patients enables student clinicians to practice proper patient and clinician positioning (e.g., adjusting operator lights, operator chair, etc.). Four dental hygiene faculty members were present for this activity, providing students with feedback from various perspectives. Students were invited to complete an anonymous survey to evaluate the experience.

RESULTS

All 16 students completed the survey.

What went well

On a scale of one to five, 15 students rated the activity a five, while one student rated the activity a four. Students noted that the exercise increased their confidence to care for clinic patients. Student patients were pleased to offer feedback to their peers.

What did not go well

All the students recommended changing the area of the mouth on each student patient during the session to avoid tissue trauma and soreness. Student feedback on the activity can be found in Table 1, which shows that 25% of participants wanted more time to practice with each patient.

Student feedback.

What lessons were learned

In response to student feedback, the following changes were made:

Multiple areas of student patient mouths will be used for practice throughout the session.

Students sit their patient back up after working on them to increase practice with patient and operator positioning.

Electronic health record documentation will be added to the activity to increase student self‐efficacy for this task.

Faculty will be assigned to stations so that students receive more equal amounts of guidance.

Students will have more time to interact with each patient in the 90‐minutes session (i.e., up to 15 minutes) by conducting the activity on two different days, with group A acting as clinicians on the first day and group B on the second day.

These changes will be applied during the Fall 2023 semester and more formal evaluation will be conducted.
